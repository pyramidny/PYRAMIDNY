What's built, and what's coming

The portal is being built in stages, and each stage leaves it working rather than half-finished. This page says what you can use today, what arrives next, and what each piece actually changes about the working day. The other two guides describe the finished workflow — this is the page that tells you which parts of them are real yet.

No dates here on purpose. The order is settled; the calendar moves with everything else going on. What is listed is the sequence and what each step unlocks.

Working today use it now

Clients, buildings and contacts

The full client → building → project drill-down. Office and site contacts, BIN and block/lot on each building, one contact across many buildings.

Projects and the 10 stages

Job numbers assigned automatically, one shared sequence for Regular and IRA. Stages advance forward and back, with the award gate on Stage 3.

Tasks and assignment

Tasks seeded per stage. Assign to a person, complete your own from My Tasks, in-app notification when something lands on you.

SharePoint folders

One folder per job, created with the job number at bid time and never moved. Documents and photos upload into the right subfolder from the project.

Tool Control

Scan a QR tag, check tools out and in, see who has what. Maintenance, tags and reports for the tool crib; catalog changes for tool admins.

Roles and access

Jorge's eight roles plus the Tool and Billing add-ons, self-serve on the Team screen. What each role can do now matches the permission matrix.

The one that matters most why the next two steps are the next two steps

The goal: no more weekly meeting to find out who owes what. Billing exports the A/R report from QuickBooks, uploads it, and every job in the portal shows what it has been invoiced, what has been paid, and how far behind it is — visible to the PM on that job without anyone being asked. Collections works from a list that is already right.

That needs two things in order, and the order is not negotiable: the portal has to know which QuickBooks job is which project before an uploaded invoice can be matched to anything. That link is what the billing queue creates.

Step 1Billing queue — the job gets its QuickBooks name recorded Step 2Upload the A/R export — invoices match themselves to jobs ResultMoney owed visible per job, routed to the right PM

This is also why three steps in the Billing runbook are badged Not built yet — they are the two steps above, written down in advance so the process is ready the day the screens are.

What's coming, in order

Billing queue and the QuickBooks handoff

Next

When a bid is won, the job appears in a Billing queue on its own — nobody has to remember to tell Billing. The card carries the client, building, contract amount, who to bill, the PM and Rep, and a link straight to the bid document. It suggests the QuickBooks job name; Billing creates the job, pastes the real path back, and the two systems are linked from then on.

What changes: Billing stops hunting through server folders for bid documents, and the same client's jobs stop getting named three different ways in QuickBooks.

A/R import and money visibility

After that

Billing runs the memorized A/R report in QuickBooks, exports it to Excel or CSV, and uploads it — about two minutes, weekly. Each invoice matches itself to its job. Anything that doesn't match lands in a short review list rather than disappearing. Aging uses QuickBooks' own 30/60/90 buckets, and the Rep on each invoice routes it to that PM's view.

What changes: the weekly billing meeting stops being how the company finds out where the money is. QuickBooks stays the source of truth for dollars — the portal just shows what it already knows, to the people who need it.
Needs two things from Pyramid, not from code: the PORTAL – A/R Aging Detail report memorized in QuickBooks by Finance, and one sample export from it so the reader is built against the real file. Both can happen now — they do not have to wait for the billing queue.

Bids workspace, scope and win/loss

Partly built

Job numbering, the permanent folder and the award handoff already work. What's still to come is the bidding crew getting their own workspace separate from production, itemised scope on a job, and marking a bid lost with a reason so win rates can be reported by client and estimator.

What changes: estimators stop sharing a view with production, and lost bids become data instead of disappearing.

Permits, insurance and expiry alerts

Later

Permits and insurance tracked as records rather than filenames, sorted by what expires first, with reminders at 90, 60 and 30 days. Expiry dates get read off an uploaded COI or permit automatically — but a person always confirms the date before it saves. Today these exist only as SharePoint folders on a job.

What changes: an expiring DOB permit or COI surfaces before it lapses instead of after.

Documents — drag-drop and editing in place

Later

Drag several files at once with a destination picker, attach documents to individual tasks, and open Word or Excel files in the browser with changes saving back to SharePoint. Uploading already works — this is convenience on top of it.

What changes: fewer trips through the folder tree. This one is the most movable in the list — it can come forward if the office wants it sooner.
