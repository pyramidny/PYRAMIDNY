Billing runbook — from awarded job to paid invoice

What to do in QuickBooks when a job is awarded, and how the portal and QuickBooks stay in step. Written for the way Pyramid actually bills — parent management company, jobs underneath, AIA pay applications on the bigger work.

Primary owner: Noemi Santos · Backup: Nina Lee-Chan · Questions: Bill Kane, app@pyramidny.com ?

Why this exists. Today the billing labels get made up fresh for each location, which is why the same client's jobs are named a few different ways in QuickBooks. This runbook fixes that by writing the naming down once. Nothing about how you invoice changes.
Read this first. This guide describes the workflow end to end, including parts of the portal that are still being built. Steps marked Not built yet or Partly built describe how it will work — don't go looking for those screens today. Everything unmarked works right now. The badges come off as each phase ships.

First — is this a new client, or a new job for a client you already bill?

Most of the time

Existing customer → Add Job

Akam, Douglas Elliman, Tri-Hill and the rest are already in QuickBooks. You do not create a new customer. You add a Job underneath the one that's already there. Skip to Step 1.

Occasionally

Brand-new company → New Customer, then Add Job

Only when Pyramid has never billed this management company before. Create the customer first, then add the job under it. Never create a customer per building.

? Why never a customer per building
Because a property manager pays the bills, not the building. Akam manages Victoria House, 805 Washington and 269 West 72nd — three buildings, one company writing the checks. If each building were its own customer, you couldn't see what Akam owes you in total, and the A/R report would scatter one relationship across three lines. Buildings are jobs underneath the company.
1

Open the billing queue item

Not built yetPortal

When a bid is won, the job lands in the portal's Billing queue on its own — you don't have to be told. Open the card. Everything you need to create the QuickBooks job is on it, and the bid document is one click away.?

This is the part that replaces the hunting. Today you go looking through the server folders for the bid docs before you can start a bill. The card carries the client, job site, contract amount, who to invoice, and a link straight to the bid document in SharePoint.
  • Client name and type
  • Job site — address, BIN, block and lot
  • Contract amount and division (Regular or IRA)
  • Who receives invoices at that client
  • PM and their Rep code
  • A suggested Customer:Job name
2

Add the job in QuickBooks

QuickBooks Enterprise

Open the Customer Center and add the job under the parent company.

Customers → Customer Center (or Ctrl + J)
→ right-click the parent customer e.g. Akam
→ Add Job

The New Job window opens with the parent's details already filled in.?

The address on the job is the job site — change it. QuickBooks pre-fills the parent company's address. Overwrite it with the building address from the queue card. Editing it here only affects this job, never the parent customer's record. That's how one Akam record ends up with three different building addresses underneath it.
3

Name the job — the important part

QuickBooks Enterprise

Use the Pyramid job number first, then the address, then the scope:

Format
P11512 - 200 East 27th Street_Facade Restoration

Which makes the full path
Akam:P11512 - 200 East 27th Street_Facade Restoration

The portal suggests this name on the queue card — you can paste it straight in.?

Why the number goes first. The job name is the one field that shows up on every A/R report automatically, so putting the number there means it's always on the export with nothing extra to remember. It also sorts the QuickBooks list the same way the job folders sort on the server — A11331, P11526 — so the two match at a glance. P.O. No. and custom fields were the alternatives, but both depend on someone filling them in every time.
Don't rename a job later. Once invoices exist against a job name, changing it breaks the match between QuickBooks and the portal. If a name is wrong, tell Bill rather than editing it — it's a two-minute fix on the portal side.
4

Fill the Job Info tab

QuickBooks Enterprise

Click the Job Info tab and fill what's useful. None of it is required for the portal to work, but it makes the QuickBooks side readable on its own.

FieldWhat to put
Job DescriptionThe scope from the queue card — e.g. Facade restoration, east and south elevations
Job TypeThe scope type — Facade Restoration, FISP Repairs, Roof Replacement
Job StatusAwarded when it's set up; In Progress once work starts
Start DateLeave blank until the job actually mobilizes?
There's normally a 2–3 month gap between a job being awarded and work being scheduled. Setting a start date at award time makes every job look overdue. Fill it in when the PM says they're mobilizing.
5

Paste the path back into the portal

Not built yetPortal

Copy the exact Customer:Job you just created and paste it into the box on the queue card, then click Mark Created in QuickBooks.

That's the whole handoff. From then on, every invoice you write against that job matches itself to the right project in the portal automatically.?

Why we ask you to paste rather than letting the app guess. The app suggests a name, but you might adjust it — a longer building name, a different scope wording. Whatever you actually type in QuickBooks is what the invoices will carry, so the portal stores your version, not its own. That one paste is what keeps the two systems from drifting apart.
6

Invoice as you do today

QuickBooks Enterprise

Nothing changes here. Progress billing, AIA pay applications, retainage — all exactly as you run it now. Two small habits worth keeping consistent:

  • Rep — set it to the project manager on the job. The portal reads this column to route each invoice to the right PM's view.?
  • P.O. No. — if the client gives you a PO number, put it here. Useful for them, harmless for us.
This one saves you the per-PM tabs. Right now the A/R export gets split into separate sheets for each PM group by hand. The portal reads the Rep column and does that automatically, so each PM sees their own jobs and nobody else's. Same result, no spreadsheet work.

The weekly A/R upload — about two minutesNot built yet

This replaces building the status PDF by hand. Once it's uploaded, the whole team sees paid, unpaid and aging on their own jobs without asking you.

  1. Reports → Memorized Reports → PORTAL – A/R Aging Detail
  2. Change the report date to today. Nothing else.
  3. Export to Excel or CSV — not PDF ?
  4. In the portal: A/R → Upload A/R report, pick the file, done.
This is the one habit change in the whole runbook. A PDF is a picture — the app can't read the numbers out of it. Excel or CSV carries the actual figures, which is what lets the team see live status instead of waiting on a report. Same report, same memorized settings, different export button.
